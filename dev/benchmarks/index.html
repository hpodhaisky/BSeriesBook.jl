Benchmarks

The code of BSeriesBook.jl follows the book line by line, and the book's representation — trees as standard numbers, B-series as plain vectors, and tables of splittings and partitions that are built once — is also fast, which matters for research computations at higher orders. This page compares it with BSeries.jl 0.1.72 and RootedTrees.jl 2.26.0 (H. Ranocha, D. I. Ketcheson).

Different scope

BSeries.jl handles more general objects (arbitrary tree types, coloured trees, symbolic coefficients, additive, Rosenbrock and multirate methods); BSeriesBook.jl in turn has general linear methods and their underlying one-step method. The comparison covers only the operations both packages offer.

Set-up

Script benchmark/compare.jl of the repository (BenchmarkTools.jl, minimum over 5–20 runs). Before each measurement the script checks that both packages give the same results. Machine: Intel Core Ultra 5 225T, one thread, Julia 1.12, 18 November 2026.

BSeriesBook times exclude building Trees(p) (first row) and the tables of splittings and partitions, which are built once per Trees object on the first call of compose and of substitute / modified_equation (their cost is listed below). The factor is the time of BSeries.jl divided by the time of BSeriesBook.jl (> 1: BSeriesBook.jl is faster).

Where the differences come from

  • Generating trees is faster in RootedTrees.jl (factor 2–4 at order 10). BSeriesBook.jl also builds the product table prod; this is the price of the standard numbering, paid once.
  • Elementary weights are 5–15 times faster: the recursion ψ(t_ℓ ∘ t_r) = ψ(t_ℓ) ⊙ φ(t_r), φ = Aψ, handles each tree with one Hadamard product and one matrix–vector product, and the results live in a vector indexed by the standard number instead of a dictionary keyed by trees.
  • Composition is 80–110 times faster: the table of splittings does not depend on the two series and is reused, so every composition is a single sum over 37 702 entries at order 10. Even including the one-off construction of the table (9.4 ms), BSeriesBook.jl is faster at order 10 than one composition in BSeries.jl.
  • Modified equation is only about twice as fast: both packages spend most of the time in Rational{BigInt} arithmetic over the partitions, not in finding them.
  • Memory at order 12 (7813 trees): the table of splittings needs about 100 MB; a dense λ matrix would need about 470 MB.
